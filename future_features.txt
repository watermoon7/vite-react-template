Target platforms for everything below: Chrome, Edge and Brave (Chromium) on Fedora, Windows and macOS.
Desktop only. Firefox is not a target.

1- Passive voice call (a Discord-style voice room)
   One always-existing voice room for the two of us, not a phone-style call. Either user can join or
   leave whenever they like; nobody is "called" and nothing rings. The left panel shows the room with
   who is currently in it (a live presence indicator next to each name), so if one of us is sitting in
   the room the other can see that and hop in. Joining asks for the microphone once, then shows an
   in-call strip with mute/unmute, leave, and connection state; leaving (or closing the tab, or losing
   the connection) removes you from the room immediately for the other person too.
   How it works: room membership is ordinary shared state in the Durable Object, pushed over the
   existing WebSocket like everything else, and it is also what drives the presence indicator. When
   both of us are in the room the two browsers open a direct 1:1 WebRTC audio connection; the DO only
   relays the connection setup (SDP offers/answers and ICE candidates) between the two sockets. WebRTC
   media is DTLS-SRTP end to end, so a direct call is encrypted with nothing extra to build; a TURN
   relay (Cloudflare Realtime TURN, credentials minted by the Worker) is needed as a fallback for
   networks that block direct connections, and it only ever sees encrypted packets. Only one tab per
   user can be in the room at a time. Same browser/OS targets as above.

2- Shared music player, in sync for everyone signed in
   A playlist both of us can see and manage: upload songs (drag and drop or a file picker, with a
   progress bar and a sensible size cap), reorder them, rename them, delete them, and see who added
   what. One shared player: play, pause, seek, next/previous, and the current position are the same
   for every signed-in user, so pressing play here starts it there. Anyone who opens the app while a
   song is playing sees it playing and joins at the right position after one click ("join listening" —
   browsers refuse to start audio without a click).
   How it works: song files are too big for the Durable Object's SQLite (2 MB per row; songs are
   3-10 MB), so they go in a Cloudflare R2 bucket (10 GB free), uploaded through and served by the
   Worker with HTTP Range support so seeking works. The playlist itself (title, order, uploader, R2
   key, duration) is a normal table in the DO like boards and tasks. Playback is one small piece of
   shared state — which song, playing or paused, and the position at a given server time — pushed to
   every client; each client works out where the song should be from the server clock (offset
   estimated over the existing WebSocket ping) and nudges its own player when it drifts more than
   ~200 ms. Expect the two players to be within roughly a tenth to a third of a second of each other:
   fine for listening together, not sample-accurate. MP3, AAC, Opus/OGG and FLAC all play on the
   target browsers.

3- Screen sharing inside the voice room, with audio
   While in the voice room either user can share their whole screen, a window, or a browser tab; the
   other sees it in the main pane (with a proper large view, and a way to stop sharing / stop
   watching). Audio should come with the share where the platform allows it (see below), on top of
   the microphones already in the call. Targets: Chrome, Edge and Brave on Fedora, Windows and macOS.
   How it works: getDisplayMedia() adds a video (and, where available, audio) track to the same
   WebRTC connection as the voice call, so this depends on item 1 being done first. On Fedora with
   Wayland the picker is the system PipeWire/portal dialog. Roughly 2-5 Mbps for a 1080p share,
   fine over a direct connection.
   What "with audio" can mean on each platform, in-browser, today (this is a browser/OS limit, not
   something the app can change):
     - Windows, Chromium: browser-tab audio and whole-screen system audio both work.
     - macOS, Chromium: browser-tab audio works; whole-screen system audio only on Chrome 141+ with
       macOS 14.2+ (older Chrome or macOS: tab audio only).
     - Fedora, Chromium: browser-tab audio only. System/application audio cannot be captured by the
       browser; the only route is an OS-level loopback device (PipeWire/PulseAudio loopback) fed in
       as the microphone, which is per-machine setup outside the app.
   Note Brave's WebRTC IP-leak protection can stop direct connections; the TURN fallback from
   item 1 covers it.
